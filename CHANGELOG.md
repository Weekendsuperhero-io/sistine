## [Unreleased]

### Changed
- **Tooling**: Vitest 4 → 5 and framer-motion 13 → 14. Neither needed code changes: the suite has no `vi.mock`, snapshots or un-awaited async assertions, and its spies are created per test, so Vitest 5's breaking changes (mocks cleared before each test, unawaited assertions failing, and the rest) do not touch it; framer-motion is not imported anywhere. `bun test` now stops with the right commands (`bun run test`, `bun run test:dom`) through a `bunfig.toml` preload: it is Bun's own runner, which ignores `vitest.config.mts` (no jsdom, no setup file), so it reported ~180 false failures such as `localStorage is not defined`. `bun run typecheck` is now `bun check` (Bun 1.4.3's type checker) instead of `tsc --noEmit`: it reads the same tsconfig and, measured here, reports exactly what tsc does (the same 204 files checked, and under `--noUncheckedIndexedAccess` the same 93 errors at the same positions with the same messages), about twice as fast (0.25s against 0.55s for tsc with its incremental cache warm). `bun check` is new in 1.4.3, so CI now reads Bun's version from `.bun-version` (1.4.3) in both the setup action and the registry refresh, instead of pinning 1.3.14 in each. And `bunfig.toml` pins the hoisted install layout: Bun 1.4 installs a fresh checkout isolated, where the stories lose `@storybook/react` (never declared; it comes with `@storybook/nextjs`) and components lose their React prop types, ~190 type errors CI would hit on 1.4.3 and no dev machine sees. `typescript` stays a dev dependency: `next build` and editors still use it.
- **Aurora by day**: the day palette is now BLUE HOUR so the lights show by day. Under the old pale golden-hour sky (L 0.76–0.85 below the zenith, plus a white cirrus veil and a horizon haze) the curtains could only sit below the sky's lightness and read as muddy bands, and the whole sky read washed out; now the upper sky is a saturated twilight blue (zenith L 0.42) and the curtains sit above it in lightness and glow, as they do at night. By day their bright lip lifts a quarter of the way up (the reflection's drops to match), so the warm horizon band and the sun's glare show beneath them, where real aurora fades into a low sun. Day rays are also crisp: a new `--au-crisp` (day 0.3, night 1) scales both the curtain blur and each ray's feathered ramp in and out of the tile (a `rays()` helper builds the three tiles), so the edges are clean and deep blue shows between the rays where the soft ramps used to merge into one pastel haze. The wide violet-pink veil layer steps back to 0.3 by day (0.75 at night), and the horizon glow strip, the cirrus and most of the waterline haze are off by day. The sun is the same banded outrun disc as every other scene: it had been made solid because its band gaps showed the curtains through the disc, and a sky-gradient patch masked to the disc (`.au-sunsky`) now sits behind it, so the gaps show sky. The day reflection is a set of straight STREAKS: with crisp rays, a plain mirror of the curtains read as identical bars ruled down the water (and a wavering, glint-broken version that briefly replaced it read as lights carried along on a belt as they swayed), so by day an inline-SVG reflection (`AuroraStreaks`) stands in for the gradient mirror. Each ray's streak leans as the mirrored ray does with a slight tilt of its own, widens toward the viewer as a reflection does in perspective, is brightest at the waterline and fades with distance, and carries a lighter core down its middle, the shine of calm water by day; the day water itself is lighter, mirroring the sky. The streaks are generated from the same ray tiles as the sky (`AU_TILES`), so each starts under its own ray, and sit in the same `.au-curtain` boxes, so they lean, sway, fade and blur in step with it. Night keeps its soft gradient mirror.
- **Muse is one fresco for both modes**: Aurora took over Muse's old day sweep, so Muse no longer keeps a night twin. Its `.dark` block is gone and one block defines the neon sheet on both pages; the light/dark toggle changes the page under the sheet, not the sheet. A single alpha has to hold on both, and the dark opaque card is the bind (the sheet's bright stops lift its L33 floor toward light text): at the old day alphas that text needed pure white, so the sheet sits at 3/4 of the way from night's alphas to day's (0.26–0.30, frosted 0.38–0.42) and the wash makes room (`--glass-wash-l` 76% → 66%, `--glass-tint-a` 0.16 → 0.1, switcher preset too), which leaves a light surface where it was and a dark one 3.5 L darker. Muse's pinned `--glass-opaque-bg` (h288 violet at L91 / L34) is dropped too, so its opaque floor derives like every jewel's: the Lightness slider now moves a Muse card, AutoForeground models the floor it actually paints, and an opaque Muse card takes the blue tint its other materials already had rather than a fixed violet. Body text: 86.6 Lc light, 92.4 dark.
- **Outline is a page-wide axis on every material**: the switcher's outline toggle used to reach opaque surfaces only (`--glass-opaque-outline`), so on any other material it did nothing. `data-glass-outline` on the root now draws `var(--glass-outline)` (default the accent) at `var(--glass-outline-w)` round every `.glass` and `[data-surface]` element, whatever the material (glass, frosted, crystal, chakra and opaque, bordered or not). The glass utility now resolves one edge (`--srf-edge` / `--srf-edge-w`) in priority order: `borderColor="ink"`, then the outline, then the material's own rim. The old `--glass-opaque-outline(-w)` names are still read as fallbacks, and the switcher keeps its storage keys; its toggle is now labelled "Outline". `check-theme` guards the wiring (`[outline-all]`).
- **Opaque carries the fresco**: the four frescoes (muse, sistine, aurora, gloaming) paint their multi-hue sheet over crystal and chakra; opaque (the material and the opaque page style) now layers the same `--glass-crystal-fresco` slot between its tint wash and floor, so a fresco page reads as one sheet whichever material a surface uses. It had been left out only because nothing modelled text contrast under the sheet. That gap affected every material (see Fixed).
- **Opaque is the glass stack at full opacity**: every other material ends its background stack in the tint wash (the solid film of the preset's colour); opaque dropped it, so its floor had to carry the tint alone and an opaque card read as a darker, greyer cousin of the glass beside it, most visibly as the dull chip in the Button materials row. Opaque (the material and the opaque page style) now paints its floor under the same `--glass-wash-layer`. The floor moves to where floor plus wash lands beside each preset's glass: L93 in light (from 88, its cap scaled by a new `--glass-opaque-cap-scale: 0.55` because the per-preset caps are L88 ceilings) and L33.4 in dark (from 36.4, with the dark chroma scale 0.98 so turquoise stays in gamut there). Measured on lapis, opaque now samples `rgb(196,207,243)` beside glass `rgb(193,206,244)` in light, and `rgb(47,66,159)` beside `rgb(61,82,184)` in dark (it was `rgb(50,61,91)`). The sheer backing keeps its own lightness in both modes (92 / 36.4; moonstone night 52), so the tint switcher's Lightness slider now moves the opaque card in dark as it already did in light, and dark glass gives up 7% of its backing chroma to the shared scale. Text stays above the 76.5 Lc margin on every opaque card (tightest destructive light 76.6, moonstone dark 76.6) and light opaque body text now reaches its full 84 aim; `--glass-border-ink-a` rises to 0.55 to keep the ink border over its non-text floor on the lighter dark cards. AutoForeground, `check-contrast` and the surface-parity and hue-match tests model the composite; `check-gamut` learns the scaled-cap shape and scores dark surfaces by a preset's own dark block.
- **Chase**: Pac-Man and the ghosts are 30% larger (1.3 pellet cells, capped at 44px), and the ghosts have painted eyes, white with pupils looking where they are going; their eyes used to be holes in the sprite, so a pellet behind one read as a stray pupil. STILL (Speed 0 or reduced motion) the maze is a frozen moment of the game instead of an empty board: each lane's runners hold a designed pose as their base transform (which the running animation overrides), Pac mid-row with his mouth open and an eaten trail behind him, his ghost ahead, the caught lanes' ghosts in fright blue just out of reach. With the animations removed they had sat at their keyframe origins, every Pac off-screen to the left and every ghost stacked in a column at the edge.
- **Clouds, refined**: by day the deck reads as cloud rather than fog. The horizon haze over it is thinner (0.75 → 0.4 alpha), the dome tops feather over 18% of their radius instead of a third (so the puffs keep their silhouettes against one another), and the shaded bellies under them sit 8–9 L deeper. The deck stays the authored white and blue-grey, with no theme cast (tinted snow reads dirty). At night the sky takes the same tint cast as the day sky (`--sw-cast`); it ignored the tint entirely, the one horizon scene that looked the same in every theme after dark.
- **Theme design fixes for consumers (BREAKING for custom toasters)**: *`@apply glass` surfaces* — the opaque page style now drops the solidify layer through a token on its own scope (`--srf-solidify: none`), so it reaches surfaces with no `.glass` class, and `glass-veil` restores the layer with a non-inheriting `--veil-solidify`; element-level page-style rules also accept a documented `data-surface` marker (`data-surface="veil"` when veiled). New invariants `[surface-marker]` and `[solidify-owner]`. *Toasts* — every `[data-sonner-toast]` rule left the theme (engine blocks, status presets, page styles and `sonner.css`); `Toaster` is now `unstyled`, draws each toast with `glassMaterial({ border, veil, size: "lg" })` and sonner's layout in classes, and stamps `data-glass-tint` from sonner's `data-type` (`error` → `destructive`), so status toasts use the theme's ordinary status blocks. Visible changes: rounder corners, status title colours apply (sonner's unlayered `color: inherit` used to beat them), status edges in their own hue, and default toasts follow the page tint. A consumer with its own Toaster must adopt the new `sonner.tsx`. New invariant `[vendor-free]`; `[status]` reads `TOAST_TINT`. *Scoped gloss* — `data-gloss` now works on any element, not just `<html>`: the gloss and the stacks that bake it are composed in a new engine block C on `:root, .dark, [data-glass-tint], [data-gloss]`, flavors are two leaf switches (`--glass-gloss-is-white` / `--glass-gloss-is-hue`), and a nested `data-glass-tint` keeps the inherited flavor instead of resetting it to tonal. New invariant `[gloss-scope]`; `check-gamut` seeds the hue flavor's L74 for its variants.
- **Chakra renames (BREAKING)**: renamed the tint presets Emerald → Aventurine, Bone → Moonstone, and Neutral → Selenite to align the jewel palette with the chakra-stone set. The demo switcher migrates stored legacy names automatically, but consumers targeting `[data-glass-tint="emerald"]` / `[data-glass-tint="bone"]` must update to the new names.
- **Material system (BREAKING)**: redesigned the glass API around four **materials** (`glass`, `frosted`, `crystal`, `opaque`) selected with a `material` prop (undefined = adaptive glass, the default; `material="none"` = plain, non-glass), composed with orthogonal **axes**, `border`, `veil` (the legibility floor for menus/overlays), `gradient`, `glow`, `sheen`, while `size` stays the component's own dimension. On raw elements the surface is the single structural `glass` utility plus the `glass-border` / `glass-veil` / `glass-sm` / `glass-lg` / `glass-gradient` / `glass-glow` / `glass-glow-lg` / `glass-sheen` axis classes, with the material set via a `data-material` attribute. Components now live in **one tree** (`components/ui/*`) and resolve their surface through `materialSurface` / `glassMaterial` in `lib/material.ts` instead of hardcoded recipe-class strings. Renamed the crystal gloss attribute `data-crystal` → `data-gloss`. The page-style `data-glass` and tint `data-glass-tint` attributes are unchanged.

### Removed
- **Legacy glass API (BREAKING)**: removed the `variant="glass|frosted|crystal|opaque|surface|solid"` surface tiers, the recipe utility classes (`glass-bg`, `glass-surface`, `glass-solid`, `glass-opaque`, `glass-frosted`, `glass-crystal`), the per-component `glass={{ color, blur, transparency, outline }}` prop, replaced by the `glassVars({ tintH, tintC, tintA, opacity, blur, solidA })` helper that emits CSS custom properties, and the `components/ui/glass/*` wrapper tree.

### Added
- **Background export (`bun run export:backgrounds`)**: writes every pattern scene as a still image (wallpapers): light and dark, every sun / moon placement on the horizon scenes, at named sizes (`desktop` 3840×2160 by default, `laptop`, `ultrawide`, `tablet`, `phone`) or any `WxH@scale`, in PNG, WebP or JPEG, for any tint presets and an optional pinned accent. `--format mp4` or `webm` writes seamless video loops of the animated scenes instead (H.264 / VP9 through ffmpeg). Time is stepped, not recorded: every CSS animation is paused and set to each frame's time, so no frame drops and the timing is exact however slow the capture. Every loop is seamless, with no crossfade in the usual case: when a scene's animations share a common cycle of at most `--max-loop` seconds (default 60) the video is exactly that cycle (synthwave and moonrise: 8s at speed 8); otherwise it is RETIMED, a whole-frame length into which every animation fits a whole number of its cycles once its speed is nudged, chosen so the largest nudge is smallest (at the defaults dune ×1.09, chase ×1.14, clouds and aurora ×1.33, forest's slowest fog ×1.6), and the factors are printed. Only past `--max-stretch` (default 1.75×) does it fall back to `--duration` seconds whose last `--fade` seconds are crossfaded into the start, which double-exposes whatever moved meanwhile. `--speed`, `--fps` and the size apply as for stills (video defaults to 1920×1080). Chrome runs with `--run-all-compositor-stages-before-draw`: raster is otherwise asynchronous, and a layer seeked into view from off-screen (a Pac sprite) was captured blank, before its tiles were painted. The scenes are live CSS and inline SVG that no in-page rasterizer paints faithfully, so `scripts/export-backgrounds.mjs` drives a real headless Chrome over the DevTools protocol, with no new dependencies (it finds Chrome, Chromium, Edge or Playwright's cached Chromium, or takes `--chrome`). It seeds theme, tint and accent into a throwaway profile, checks that each frame actually rendered them, and writes to the git-ignored `exports/backgrounds/<tint>/`. The default run is 46 images in about 70 seconds against `bun run dev`. It photographs a new `/backdrop` route: one scene, full-viewport, picked by the query (`?scene=forest&disc=left&density=…&sand=…&speed=…`), in the visitor's own theme and tint, so the URL also works as a live wallpaper. The forest scene's SVG ids are now scoped per instance (`useId`), because `url(#…)` resolves to the first match in the document, so a second forest on a page painted with the first one's gradients.
- **`forest` pattern scene**: a coastal redwood forest seen from inside it, standing on a hill, at sunrise by day and under the moon by night. Dense here is about where you stand, not how many trees there are: the near ridge holds a few giants whose crowns climb out of the top of the frame on massive, buttressed red trunks, and each of the four ridges behind holds more, smaller trees with fewer tiers, receding by aerial perspective into marine-layer fog that pools under every ridge (no horizon line). The three nearest ridges leave a CLEARING in their trees and are shifted by the sun / moon placement so it always sits under the disc: you look out at the sun or moon through a gap in the giants, with the hill dipping into it, while the ridges behind the giants stand densest right around the disc (their clearings are narrow, so their crowns crowd in and overlap its edges). The trees take dune's cut, faceted look: every crown is a stack of tiers, each a triangle with a FLAT underside and upper edges that SCOOP in, so the green sweeps out to its tips like a drooping bough, each overlapping the one below by more than half its height so a DEEP CUT runs almost to the trunk between tiers; the outline stays straight-sided, columnar through the lower crown and then tapering to a spire or, on a quarter of the trees (old growth), to a flat top. Trees vary in how deep their branches scoop, which side reaches further and how strongly their tiers alternate long and short, and each is TURNED: the line between its lit and shadow sides runs off the trunk axis, so it reads as a cone angled toward or away from the viewer rather than a symmetric cut-out. (On the way there: an underside rising to a point at the trunk stacked every tier into a column of chevrons, a convex domed outline read as puffy, and a multi-spike old-growth top read as a tree with two heads that repeated across the screen.) Every tier splits at the trunk into two facets, shadow and lit, and the lit side follows the sun / moon placement control the way dune's faces follow `--du-light`; for depth each tier casts a translucent shadow band onto the tier below (a light and a mid green on the lit side, two deeper ones in shadow), clipped to the crowns so it never hangs off a deep-cut tip into the sky, kept to the width of the tier it shades with its ends tapered as under a cone, and skipped where a narrow spire tier sits on a broad one (there it floated mid-crown as a dark bar). Trunks are rounded, with a curved buttress flare and a darker strip down each edge (a four-band cylinder with their lit and shadow halves), and striped with deep red-brown vertical FURROWS, dune's ridge striation, that splay into the buttress; the ground is angular and stays BROWN at every distance (a stylistic choice: the trees fade into the haze, but each floor shades from its duff brown to a deeper brown, the trunks fade toward it rather than into fog, and the drifting fog that crosses the floor is thinned so it doesn't wash it out), with dune's sand detail in forest form: a lit rim along the near crests and, on the near floor, a litter of wood streaks and duff specks in browns only (no needles: short dashes at every slant read as green pine needles on the floor), drawn as two SVG patterns of unrelated cell sizes (as dune runs two striation passes at different periods) so neither reads as rows. The disc sits behind the trees, by day crepuscular rays break through the fog in the clearing, and motion is three fog strips drifting on compositor-only loops, stopped by `stopRules` like every scene. The day palette stays mid-tone (nearest shadow facet L 0.58) because light-mode hero copy sits on the giants' crowns. Unlike the other scenes, the trees are INLINE SVG rather than CSS masks: one `<svg>` per ridge, its tile drawn once and placed side by side with `<use>`, each part filled by a gradient whose stop colours read the palette variables. As 63 full-viewport masked layers, a light/dark flip re-composited every mask and took ~1.2s in Safari against dune's ~0.2s; as SVG it only refills paths (~65ms against dune's ~45ms in headless Chromium). Each ridge deals its trees into layers in turn, painted back to front (three on the dense ridges, where a crown reaches the tree after its neighbour), since each part is one path with no stacking order inside it and overlapping trees in one layer merged; on ridges without a clearing the tree count is a multiple of the layers, so neighbours across a tile repeat land in different layers too. The geometry comes from a seeded generator, so the forest is identical on server and client, and whole-cycle ridges with trees simply overlapping into the next tile make every ridge repeat seamlessly (about 146 KB of markup while the scene is active, roughly 31 KB gzipped). Tests pin determinism, seamless tiling, that every palette colour a ridge paints with is defined in both modes, that the clearing ridges are anchored to the disc, and that the facets facing the disc are the lit ones. Also: the shared moon and sun now round their size to whole pixels (behind `@supports`, since `round()` reached Chrome only in 125), because WebKit clipped a flat sliver off a filtered disc at fractional widths.
- **Seven shadcn items, material-aware**: `kbd` (theme tokens and the glass hairline instead of a surface, no tooltip inversion), `empty` (shadcn's anatomy; plain by default, `variant="glass"` for its own surface, a bordered chip for `EmptyMedia variant="icon"`; supersedes the legacy `empty-state`), `progress` (Slider's track and `--glass-accent` fill), `item` (a surface role per variant: plain `default`, bordered-glass `outline`, borderless-glass `muted`), `field` (plain layout, with shadcn's dead `has-data-checked:` / `group-has-data-horizontal/field:` variants fixed and the separator drawn as two rules instead of a `bg-background` knock-out), `menubar` (Radix Menubar; a bordered glass bar whose menus and sub menus are portaled, veiled glass with the popper snap and `bg-accent` rows; the legacy `menu-bar` story is now `Sistine/MenuBarLegacy`) and `combobox` (Base UI, as shadcn now uses, via the new `@base-ui/react@^1.9.0` dependency; veiled glass popup, a bordered small-tier chips field, and shadcn's never-matching `data-open:data-closed:animate-out` fixed). Each ships as a registry item with a docs preview, a code example, a story and tests (`menubar`, `combobox`, `field`, `progress`, `item`). `form` and `split-pane` were not adopted: Field with a form library's Controller covers the first, `resizable` the second.
- **`borderColor="ink"` axis**: the material edge is a near-white rim at the material's alpha, which reads as glass on a dark page and as nothing on most light themes at any weight, so `border="rim" | "frame"` had no visible use there. `borderColor="ink"` (class `glass-border-ink`) draws the border in the element's own text colour, `--foreground` at `--glass-border-ink-a` (0.5), through `MaterialProps`, `splitAxisProps`, `glassMaterial` and role defaults in `materialSurface`; it also colours the opaque page's uniform panel edge. `--brd-ink` is registered non-inheriting, so an inked panel does not ink the borders inside it. `check-contrast` gains an `[ink]` check that the line clears the APCA non-text floor (30 Lc) on the sheer and opaque surface of every preset in both modes (tightest 31.6); `tests/material.test.tsx` pins the class output, role inheritance and that the prop never reaches the DOM.
- **ARC Bronze contrast**: introduced APCA contrast banding targeting for size-tiered text foregrounds and icons.

None of the three draws a horizon *line*: the sky dissolves into the ground across a mottled haze band, and distant layers wash toward the sky's own value. ### Fixed
- **Aurora's horizon looked drawn on at night**: a cyan rule ran along the waterline, through the dark gaps between the curtains as much as under them. It was several things meeting on the line. Two full-width glow strips (above the line, and its reflection below) and the haze band all peaked exactly on it, the same at every x; both strips are gone, and the haze is flat-topped and thinner. The sky curtains ended hard 1vh past the line and showed through the water sheet as it faded in (a bright strip with a step under it), and the reflection's own blur ate its first few px (a dark one); both sets of curtains now run 3vh past the line, the sky ones clipped exactly on it and the mirror ones by the water box, so each is at full strength right to the line, and their skew pivots mirror each other through it. The water sheet's edge, meant to sit on the line, sat a quarter-percent above it (8% of its height rather than 4vh), and the ripples started at full strength on it (a 1px highlight on the line, a dark stripe 14px under it); the sheet is pinned in vh and the ripples fade in over 6vh below the line, on a still wrapper so the fade does not drift with them. Across the line, measured: night 113 → 111, day 170 → 169, no step, ridge or dip. One seam was left that an averaged profile cannot show: the reflection is blurred more than the sky (×1.5 against ×1.15), so where they meet every column changed profile, bright ones stepping down and the gaps between them stepping up, which averages out but drew a hard edge along the horizon on the static night scene (6.5 levels per column across one px in Safari, against 0.3–0.5 between other rows). At night the reflection now takes the sky's blur (`--au-mirror-blur-m`), and the step at the line is the same as between any two rows in WebKit (0.4) and Chromium (0.7). By day the softer mirror is unchanged: its reflection starts below the line, where it never meets the sky.
- **Chase: eaten pellets grew back**: Pac's wake, which erases the pellets he eats, was a 34vw gradient that rode behind him, so the dots reappeared right behind him and the next pass ate them again. It is now a strip from his mouth back past the left edge, on its own runner in step with his, that fades only once he is off-screen, so the row stays eaten for the whole pass and refills between passes. Every wake now sits in a layer below every sprite, so a Pac or ghost crossing another lane is no longer painted over by that lane's wake.
- **Synthwave and moonrise floors hitched once per loop**: the floor scrolls one grid cell per loop, `4vmin`, which is fractional on most windows (28.8px at 720px tall), while the gradient tile is painted at whole pixels, so each loop ended a fraction of a pixel off where it began and the floor jumped, magnified by its tilt. The cell is now rounded to a whole pixel (`round(4vmin, 1px)` where supported), so the scroll lands exactly on the tile. Found by the video export, which measures every frame step: the wrap step was larger than any other step in the loop, and is now in line with them.
- **Moving the sun / moon tore it in Safari**: changing the left / center / right placement on any horizon scene left the old disc's top half painted at the old spot and drew only the lower half at the new one. The disc carries a drop-shadow filter and a mask, and WebKit repainted only part of its old and new areas when its `left` changed. The shared sun and moon now get their own compositing layer (`will-change: transform`), so a move just repositions the layer.
- **Scoped tints lost their night overrides**: preset and fresco dark blocks were written as compound selectors (`.dark[data-glass-tint="lapis"]`), which only match when `.dark` and the tint sit on the same element, i.e. a page-wide tint. A preset scoped inside a dark page (`<section data-glass-tint="lapis">`) never matched them and kept its light-mode values for the dark-only tweaks (lapis's tint alpha, moonstone's lifted night floor, each fresco's night sheet). All 17 dark blocks (13 presets, 4 frescoes) now also list the descendant form (`.dark [data-glass-tint="lapis"]`), and `check-theme` fails any night block without its twin (`[night-scope]`).
- **A fresco page's sheet leaked into scoped single-hue tints**: `--glass-crystal-fresco` was declared only by the fresco blocks and inherits, so a `<section data-glass-tint="lapis">` on a muse page painted muse's sweep over its crystal, chakra (and now opaque) surfaces. Engine block A resets the slot to `none` at every tint scope; the fresco blocks, later in source order, set it back.
- **Text under a fresco was banded against the plain surface**: AutoForeground solved text against the surface without the fresco sheet, so wherever a stop pulled the surface toward the text's own polarity body text dipped to 66–74 Lc. - **Dark opaque cards were throttled by the light-mode caps**: each jewel pins `--glass-opaque-c-max` to its hue's sRGB ceiling at the LIGHT floor (L88), in an unscoped preset block that ties `.dark`'s 0.12 guard on specificity and won on source order. So in dark the seven blue/purple/red jewels rendered their opaque floor (and the dark solidify backing, which shares it) at 23–53% of the chroma their hue holds at L36.4: amethyst C 0.065 instead of its 0.106 ask, lapis 0.055 instead of 0.089. The popper snap also resets the `will-change: transform` Floating UI writes at DPR ≥ 1.5, the actual cause of soft popper text in WebKit (it composites the wrapper and drops subpixel antialiasing).
- **Pattern scenes stop instead of pausing**: reduced motion and Speed 0 now both REMOVE a scene's animations (`animation: none; will-change: auto`) through one exported helper, `stopRules()`, keyed on `prefers-reduced-motion` and a `data-pat-static` attribute on the scene root. The old `--pat-play` / `animation-play-state: paused` knob left every layer animating, so WebKit kept re-rendering six full-viewport blurred, masked layers at Speed 0, and its `will-change` resolved to the invalid `auto, auto`. Ported from the first consumer's fork with its `tileScale` prop and the `TILE_SCALE` / `GEOMETRIC_TILE` exports (so a small preview can draw several dots or grid cells). Pinned by `tests/pattern-background.test.tsx`; `check-patterns` now fails on `animation-play-state` / `--pat-play` and on any animated scene stylesheet that does not call `stopRules(`.
- **Body text no longer trades its hue for an unreadable sliver of contrast**: `pickTonalInBand`'s legibility guard handed back the raw ramp's black end whenever the tonal pick missed the 75 Lc body floor, even when black missed it too. On lapis over a darker solidify backing that meant `#000002` for 1.8 Lc. Now a tonal pick within `TONAL_HUE_SLACK_LC` (3) of the floor is kept unless the fallback actually clears the floor; a fallback that clears it still always wins, and the mid-tone dead zone (tonal ~43 Lc) still takes the extreme, so `check-contrast` is unchanged. Reproduced first in `tests/auto-foreground-lapis.test.tsx`: the shipped L92 backing was never affected (L20 renders `#09014e` at 79.7 Lc, 78.8 at `--glass-opacity: 0.8`). The consumer's second hunk, dropping ramp steps by measured luminance, is not ported: it removes exactly the L20 step that keeps lapis ink-blue.
- **Blurry text on popper surfaces**: `dropdown-menu`, `popover`, `tooltip`, `select`, `hover-card` and `context-menu` are positioned by an inline `transform` that Radix does not round. Combined with the glass `backdrop-filter` (which promotes the surface to its own compositing layer), a fractional offset rasterized every glyph off the device pixel grid. A new `lib/snap-popper.ts` rounds the wrapper's translate to whole pixels and re-applies after each reposition. Same defect the dialogs had; there the fix was to centre without a transform, which a popper can't do because its position *is* the transform.
- **Moonstone night read as brown**: at the default opacity every surface (cards, inputs, the nav pill) paints `--glass-opaque-bg`, and moonstone's dark floor derived `oklch(0.8 0.047 75)`, a sand/tobacco tan rather than the pale silvery stone the preset is named for. Lightness alone could not fix it: h75 is a warm hue, so every lightness along it lands somewhere in the tan family; cutting the CHROMA is what moves it off that family. `.dark[data-glass-tint="moonstone"]` now sets `--glass-opaque-l: 84` (from 80: the "slightly lighter" half) plus `--glass-opaque-c-scale: 0.45`, deriving `oklch(0.84 0.021 75)`. Contrast improves rather than regresses: all five static `--foreground-*-opaque` tiers gain **+7.1 Lc** against the lighter floor, and `--foreground-strong-opaque` now clears the APCA body threshold at 75.0. Light mode is untouched (still `L 94` / `c-scale 1`, where the same hue reads as cream). The wash knobs are unchanged, so the `[moonstone-sync]` mirror in `auto-foreground.tsx` needs no update.
- **Gradient and canvas backdrops were unreadable at one end**: the ramps in `lib/oklch-utils.ts` deliberately cover their FULL range, which is what makes them useful as swatches; as a full-bleed wallpaper it meant `linear-gradient` ran from pure black (L 0) to pure white (L 100) across the viewport, so a single solved foreground could only be legible at one end. Backdrops now fit their ramp into `readableLightnessBand()`: the lightness range where text over them clears APCA 60, binary-searched against the live `--foreground` (so it tracks AutoForeground, the tint and the mode). Measured: light mode bands to L 83–93, dark to L 7–63. The band stops short of L 0 / L 100 on the high-contrast end, which costs no readability and keeps the tint from washing out to pure black or white at one edge. `rampAxisColors` is untouched, so the docs swatches still show the full range.
- **Conic gradients had a hard seam**: a conic wraps, so whatever sits at 360° butts straight into 0°. An open ramp met its own opposite end at the twelve-o'clock line and drew a hard edge through the wallpaper, at full range, white against black (measured seam ΔL = 1.0). Conic now closes the loop: the cyclic `hue` axis repeats its first color at 360°, the rest mirror out and back, and the center plateau is dropped since it reads as a lopsided wedge once bent into a circle. Seam ΔL/ΔC/ΔH now measure 0. Frescoes bypass the ramp, so they get the same clamp and loop-close applied directly.
- **Tint chroma went uncontrolled on the extreme surfaces**: sRGB's chroma ceiling depends on BOTH lightness and hue, so one `--glass-tint-c` scalar could not land equally across 17 presets. Two places asked far past what any hue can hold and let the browser's gamut mapping decide the result: the near-white sheet/border/crystal stack at L 95–100% (lapis delivered 9% of its declared chroma there where peridot delivered 98%), and `--glass-gloss-ink`, whose ×4.25 multiplier is calibrated for the default tint and reached `C=0.6375` on a preset, roughly double the maximum chroma sRGB holds at *any* hue. Added `--glass-tint-c-hi` (`min(var(--glass-tint-c), 0.025)`) for the 13 near-white declarations and clamped the gloss product to `0.3`. The default tint (0.018) sits under both and is unchanged; measured border chroma across every preset is 0–0.0015 either way, which matches the engine's own note that the sheet is too sheer to show a hue, the color comes from `--glass-tint-wash`, which is untouched. This is the CSS-side counterpart to the 3% margin `lib/oklch-utils` already carried for the computed path.

### Fixed
- **Popper observers were rebuilt on every render**: the six popper surfaces composed their refs with `ref={composeRefs(ref, snapRef)}` written inline, which hands React a new callback identity each render. React responds by detaching the old ref (calling it with `null`) and attaching the new one, and that detach runs `useSnappedPopper`'s cleanup, so the `MutationObserver` watching the popper wrapper was torn down and rebuilt on *every* render of *every* popper. Added `useComposedRefs` (same shape as Radix's own: `useCallback` over the composed callback with the refs as the dependency array) and moved all **8** call sites across the six components onto it. Kept local rather than importing `@radix-ui/react-compose-refs`, since these ship through the registry and a direct dependency would have to be declared on all six.
- **`DialogContent` gains `bodyClassName`**: the inner scroll wrapper added for the overflow fix forced `grid gap-4` onto every dialog body, and `className` targets the outer surface, so consumers had no way to reach it. `bodyClassName` styles the wrapper: swap the grid for flex, drop the gap, or take the scrolling over entirely.
- **Colour picker default ignored keyboard users**: the themed hex was applied on `onPointerDown`, which never fires for someone tabbing to the swatch and pressing Space/Enter; they opened the picker on a stale value. Moved to `onFocus`, which precedes activation on both paths.

### Security
- **PR review workflow no longer inherits every secret**: `.github/workflows/pr-review.yml` used `secrets: inherit` on a job that triggers on `synchronize`, so every push to a PR branch ran the reusable workflow with the repository's full secret set. Now passes only the three the review agents actually need, `JULES_API_KEY`, `JULES_PR_CLIENT_ID`, `JULES_PR_PRIVATE_KEY`, explicitly, so adding a secret to the repo cannot silently widen this workflow's blast radius.
- **Reusable workflow pinned to a SHA**: it referenced `platform-tools/.github/workflows/reusable-pr-review.yml@main`, a mutable ref that runs with this repository's token and secrets; any push to that repo's default branch would execute here unreviewed. Pinned to `72bbd12`, with the branch and date kept as a trailing comment.
- **Popper surfaces scaled from their centre**: `popover`, `tooltip`, `select` and `hover-card` play a `zoom-in-95` entrance but never set `transform-origin`, so they grew from their own middle instead of from the trigger that opened them. Each now reads Radix's own `--radix-<component>-content-transform-origin`, matching what `dropdown-menu` and `context-menu` already did. `select` sets the variable only in `position="popper"` (its default); under `item-aligned` the variable is undefined, `transform-origin` computes to its initial `50% 50%`, and that mode keeps centre-scaling, which is what it wants.
